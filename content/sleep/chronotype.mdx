Chronotype is the timing your body clock prefers, independent of what your
calendar wants. It is distributed across a population roughly like height —
most people sit somewhere in the middle, with genuine extremes at both
ends.[^roenneberg2007] It has a substantial genetic component, it shifts later
through adolescence and earlier again with age, and it is not a matter of
discipline.

That last point is worth dwelling on, because late chronotypes get a lot of
moralising aimed at them. Being unable to fall asleep at ten is not a character
flaw any more than being unable to sleep past five is.

## Social jetlag

The measurable cost of a mismatch is what Till Roenneberg named social jetlag:
the gap between the sleep timing your body wants and the sleep timing your
working week enforces. You see it in the difference between your weekday and
weekend sleep. If you wake at half past six on weekdays and half past nine at
weekends, your body is running about three hours behind the schedule you are
keeping — the same as flying three time zones every Monday and back every
Friday.

## What actually moves the clock

Light, mostly. The circadian system is sensitive to it at levels far lower than
people assume, and timing determines direction: light in the morning pulls your
clock earlier, light in the evening pushes it later.[^zeitzer2000c]

If you want to shift earlier, the highest-leverage change is bright light soon
after waking — outdoors if you can, because even an overcast day is far brighter
than a well-lit room — combined with dimming the house in the evening. Move in
small steps, fifteen to twenty minutes at a time, and hold each step for several
days before the next.

## The limits

You can typically move your timing by an hour or two and hold it. You cannot
generally convert a genuine evening type into a morning type, and the attempt
usually produces someone who gets up early and sleeps badly.

If you are a late chronotype in a job with an early start, the honest advice is
partly practical and partly not about sleep at all: protect the morning light,
protect the evening dark, keep the schedule consistent across all seven days,
and where you have any influence over your working hours, use it. Where sleep
timing is severely and persistently out of step — for example, being reliably
unable to sleep before three or four in the morning — that may be a circadian
rhythm sleep-wake disorder rather than a preference, and it is worth raising
with a doctor.
