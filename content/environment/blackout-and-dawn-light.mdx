## The night end

Light at night suppresses melatonin and shifts circadian timing, and the
literature on artificial light at night documents associations with a range of
health outcomes — though much of it is observational and confounded by shift
work, which is difficult to separate out.[^cho2015]

For the bedroom specifically the practical picture is simpler than the
epidemiology. Street lighting through thin curtains, a bright standby LED, and a
phone screen lighting the room at three in the morning are all avoidable.

Blackout blinds are the reliable fix where outdoor light is the problem. A
well-fitted sleep mask is the cheap version and works for most people. Tape over
standby lights costs nothing.

One caveat worth stating: total blackout means no dawn signal, and for some
people that makes waking harder. If you find you sleep well but wake groggy in a
completely dark room, that trade may be the cause.

## The morning end

The same sensitivity that works against you at night is the strongest tool you
have in the morning. Light soon after waking anchors your body clock, and
outdoor light — even under heavy cloud — is far brighter than any domestic
lighting.

If you cannot get outside, dawn simulation is the studied alternative. Artificial
dawn has been found to improve subjective well-being and some measures of
morning performance, though effects are modest and studies are
small.[^gabel2013] The honest summary is that it is a reasonable option for
dark winter mornings and not a substitute for going outdoors.

## The asymmetry worth noticing

Most people who take sleep seriously invest in the night end — blackout, masks,
dim lamps — and do nothing about the morning. That is the wrong way round if
your problem is timing rather than duration.

Morning light pulls your clock earlier and strengthens the amplitude of the
rhythm. If you struggle to fall asleep at a reasonable hour, ten minutes outside
after waking is a more direct intervention than anything you do at eleven at
night. See [chronotype](/sleep/timing-and-chronotype/chronotype).
