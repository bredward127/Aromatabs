## Coherent breathing

Equal inhale and exhale, around five to six breaths per minute. No holds. Five
in, five out, or six and six.

This is the one with the clearest mechanistic backing. Breathing at roughly this
rate hits the resonance frequency of the baroreflex, where heart rate
variability amplitude peaks — the effect that heart rate variability biofeedback
is built on.[^lehrer2014b] The broader slow-breathing literature points the same
way.[^zaccaro2018c]

Use it when the job is to come down: after something stressful, before sleep, or
as a daily practice of five or ten minutes. It is undramatic, which is a feature.
You can do it for twenty minutes without discomfort, which you cannot say for
4-7-8.

If you want to find your own resonance rate rather than assuming six, work down
in half-breath steps from six to four-and-a-half and notice where the breathing
feels easiest and most settled. That is a rough version of what biofeedback
protocols establish formally.

## Box breathing

Equal counts on all four phases: inhale four, hold four, exhale four, hold four.

Box breathing has a different character. The holds make it demanding enough to
occupy your attention, which is much of the point — it originated in operational
settings as a way of staying regulated while doing something difficult, not as a
relaxation practice.

At four seconds per phase it runs at just under four breaths per minute, below
the resonance range. So if the goal is maximum parasympathetic effect, coherent
breathing is the better tool. If the goal is to hold yourself steady in a
situation where you cannot afford to drift, the structure of box breathing is
what you want.

## Choosing

**Before sleep, or to come down:** coherent breathing, longer exhale if you like.

**Before or during something hard:** box breathing.

**When you need something to work in sixty seconds:** a couple of double inhales
followed by long exhales — the cyclic sighing pattern — is the fastest-acting
option with reasonable support behind it.

None of these is a treatment. If breathlessness or a racing heart is happening
without an obvious trigger, that is a matter for a doctor rather than a
technique.
