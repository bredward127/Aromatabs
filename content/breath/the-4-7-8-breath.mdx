Inhale through the nose for four. Hold for seven. Exhale through the mouth,
audibly, for eight. Four rounds.

It is popular for good reasons: it is easy to remember, it requires nothing, and
most people notice something the first time they try it.

## Being straight about the evidence

There is very little research on this specific pattern. What exists is small and
often uncontrolled. Anyone citing a body of evidence for 4-7-8 in particular is
overstating it.

What there is evidence for is the class it belongs to: slow breathing with a
prolonged exhale, which reliably lowers heart rate and shifts autonomic balance
toward parasympathetic dominance.[^russo2017b][^zaccaro2018b] 4-7-8 is a
specific and fairly aggressive member of that class.

## Why the ratio probably matters less than advertised

A full round takes nineteen seconds, which is roughly three breaths per minute —
slower than the resonance range of about six per minute where heart rate
variability effects peak. So 4-7-8 is not optimised for the mechanism usually
invoked to explain it.

What it does have is a long exhale relative to the inhale, and a hold that
forces the whole cycle down to a rate you would not otherwise reach. Both push
in the right direction. The exact numbers are almost certainly not the active
ingredient.

## Practical notes

Four rounds is the standard prescription, and it is a reasonable place to stop
when you are new to it — the hold is demanding and doing many rounds at once
tends to make people light-headed.

If seven seconds of holding is uncomfortable, scale the whole thing down while
keeping the proportions. Two-three-and-a-half-four is the same pattern at a
gentler rate, and a pattern you can do calmly beats one you have to brace for.

If you want a technique for falling asleep specifically, the long audible exhale
seems to be what people respond to; if the hold is the part you dread, drop it
and simply exhale for twice as long as you inhale.

Skip breath-holding entirely if you are pregnant, have a cardiovascular or
respiratory condition, or become dizzy. And never practise breath-holding in
water.
