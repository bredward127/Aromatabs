Your heart does not beat like a metronome. The interval between beats varies
constantly, and the pattern of that variation carries information about
autonomic regulation. That is heart rate variability.

Higher HRV at rest is, in general and across populations, associated with better
cardiovascular health and better regulatory capacity. That association is
solid.[^shaffer2017]

What follows from it for you personally, this morning, is much less clear.

## The measurement problem

HRV is extremely sensitive to conditions. Posture changes it. Breathing rate
changes it dramatically — which is precisely the point of
[slow breathing](/breath/physiology/how-slow-breathing-works), and also why a
reading taken while you breathe differently is not comparable. Time of day,
recent food, alcohol, caffeine, hydration and ambient temperature all move it.

Researchers control for these carefully, and the methodological guidance in the
field is explicit that uncontrolled measurement produces numbers that cannot be
interpreted.[^laborde2017] A wearable measuring overnight is doing better than a
spot check, but it is still not a controlled measurement.

## Between-person versus within-person

This is the distinction that most consumer HRV coverage collapses.

Comparing your HRV to someone else's is close to meaningless. The between-person
range is enormous and largely determined by age, genetics and physiology you do
not control. A "low" HRV relative to a population norm tells you very little.

Comparing your HRV to your own baseline is more defensible, and it is what the
better wearables attempt. Even then, the day-to-day noise is large relative to
the signal, and a single low morning is not information.

## What we would suggest

If you have a wearable, look at trends across weeks rather than days, and treat
a single reading as noise. Expect alcohol, illness, and a late heavy meal to
show up. Expect stress to show up unreliably.

And be alert to the loop. If a recovery score is making you anxious about
whether you are recovered, the score has become a stressor, and you have added
a variable rather than measured one.
