Homeostasis is the idea that the body defends a set point. Allostasis is a
refinement of it: stability achieved _through change_, by systems that
anticipate demand and adjust in advance rather than correcting after the
fact.[^mcewen2003]

Allostatic load is the bill for running those systems too hard, for too long.

## Four ways it accumulates

McEwen's account identifies distinct patterns, which is more useful than a
single dial of "too much stress".[^mcewen1998b]

**Repeated hits.** Frequent novel stressors, each producing a full response.

**Failure to habituate.** The same stressor producing the same full response
every time, when it should be attenuating. The public-speaking response that
never settles after the fiftieth talk.

**Failure to shut off.** The response continuing after the stressor has gone.
This is where perseverative cognition does its damage.

**Inadequate response.** The opposite failure — an under-active system, where
other mediators overshoot because the usual brake is not applied.

The third and fourth are the ones people do not expect, and they are why
"lowering cortisol" is a poor treatment goal. A flattened curve is not a
healthy one.

## What it is measured by

Allostatic load indices typically combine markers across systems:
cardiovascular, metabolic, inflammatory and neuroendocrine. Higher composite
scores are associated with worse health outcomes across a range of large
cohort studies.

We should be honest about the limits. There is no single agreed index, the
components vary between studies, and much of the evidence is observational —
which means confounding by socioeconomic circumstance, which is itself a major
driver of chronic stress, is difficult to rule out entirely.

## Why the concept is still worth having

Because it reframes the target. The goal is not less stress. It is complete
responses: on when needed, off afterwards, and habituating to the things that
recur.

That is a goal you can actually work toward, and it is the subject of
[what recovery requires](/stress/recovery/what-recovery-requires).
