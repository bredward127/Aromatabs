The standard version goes: screens emit blue light, blue light suppresses
melatonin, therefore no screens before bed. Each step is defensible. The
conclusion is stronger than the chain supports.

## What the research shows

The most-cited study gave participants either a light-emitting e-reader or a
printed book for four hours before bed, over consecutive evenings in a
controlled laboratory. The e-reader group took longer to fall asleep, had
suppressed melatonin, showed delayed circadian timing, and were sleepier the
next morning.[^chang2015]

That is a real effect, carefully measured. Note the conditions: four hours, at
maximum brightness, held close to the face, in an otherwise dim room. It
establishes that screen light _can_ do this. It does not establish that twenty
minutes on a phone at low brightness does it meaningfully.

The circadian system's light sensitivity is well characterised, and it is
dose-dependent — intensity, duration and timing all matter.[^zeitzer2000e] A
phone at low brightness is a small light source. The ceiling light in the same
room is usually a larger one, and nobody campaigns against ceiling lights.

## What about night mode?

Shifting a screen's colour temperature reduces its short-wavelength output, so
in principle it should reduce the effect. Trials of these features have produced
small and inconsistent results, and several found no meaningful benefit for
sleep. We would not tell you to turn it off. We would not expect much from it
either.

## What is probably doing more of the work

Content and timing, not photons. A screen is a device for supplying your brain
with things to think about, at exactly the point in the evening when it needs
fewer of them. Work email raises arousal. An argument in a comment thread raises
arousal. An episode that ends on a cliffhanger keeps you up another
forty-five minutes.

## What we would actually suggest

Lower the brightness, and lower the room. Decide what the screen is for before
you pick it up. Keep work off it after your cut-off. And if you are going to
change one thing about evening light, change the overhead lighting first — it is
a bigger source and a cheaper fix.
